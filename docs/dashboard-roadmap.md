# Dashboard roadmap — what to add, per dashboard

Derived from 4 personas (Senior Eng Manager, Senior PM, Team Lead, Developer) reviewing each dashboard's real source, then reconciled into one spec per dashboard.

> **Global constraint (user):** no view/lens/role switcher anywhere. No "viewing as manager / dev" toggles, no persona modes. Where a spec proposed one, it is listed as rejected in that dashboard's **Cut** section and the panels underneath it ship as one single page.

---

## Claude Code Dashboard

*(main shell) — `src/App.jsx` + `Overview.jsx`, `Hub.jsx` and the `*Section.jsx` set, served by `server.mjs` + `server-eng.mjs`*

**Thesis** — One shell, two data planes, three lenses. The dashboard's job is: tell me what needs a human decision today, and prove whether the work (and the AI spend behind it) is paying off. It stops being a scoreboard for one laptop and becomes an attention router. The two planes are non-negotiable and structural: WORK ARTIFACTS (JIRA tickets, GitHub PRs, reviews, CI, bugs — already visible to everyone on the team, therefore safe to show per-person) and HARNESS TELEMETRY (transcripts, tokens, cost, session hours — one machine's private data, therefore self-only, forever, with no endpoint that can ingest anyone else's). Every panel declares which plane it draws from, and no panel may join across the boundary in a way that attributes harness telemetry to a named human other than the viewer. On top of those planes sit three lenses selected by one toggle that persists in `~/.claude/dashboard-meta.json`: Now (lead/EM — what is stuck, act on it), Plan (PM/EM — what did we commit, what will land, where did the quarter go), Me (IC — what is my harness costing me, what failed, get me back into that session). It must not alienate the IC: the moment this app looks like it is watching engineers, they route around it and every number in it dies. That is why the gamification layer goes (a token-count level is a leaderboard waiting to happen), why after-hours signals are team-aggregate with min-N=5 and no drilldown, and why every per-person card is built only from artifacts that person can already open about themselves.

> *Note: the "three lenses / one toggle" mechanism in this thesis is **rejected** by the user constraint above — the two data planes stand, the lens switcher does not. See Cut.*

### Consensus

- Feed real delivery risk into the existing Inbox — all of EM, PM and Lead independently asked for it, and the badge, 60s poll, desktop notification and Slack webhook are already built and currently carry nothing but harness trivia (`server.mjs:2203-2254`)
- Kill the gamification layer (Pilot Level, XP bar, streak flame, 10 achievement badges, the Lv/🔥 topbar chip) — all four personas, unprompted, asked for the same thing. XP is literally all-time assistant message count, so the way to level up is to have long thrashing conversations
- Replace Overview's hero row with delivery/attention tiles — EM, PM and Lead all said the landing page answers none of their questions; IC said the same tiles he cares about (cost, failures) are also absent
- A PR review-aging queue with an SLA threshold and a nudge action — EM, PM and Lead all named it; `firstReviewDays`/`openDays`/`reviewEvents` are already computed in `fetchPRs()` and buried as sortable columns in EngDashboard
- Fold `?dash=eng` into the main shell — all four called the four-shell split the defect: the only team-wide data in the repo is behind the chip nobody clicks
- Cut "cache saved $" as a headline KPI — EM, PM and IC: an estimate of money never spent, against a counterfactual that never happened, that only ever goes up
- Demote the static frontmatter score (Setup health ring, Level/Specificity columns, Quality distribution) — EM, PM and IC: it is a linter cosplaying as a metric
- Persist delivery history and export a narrative (weekly update / QBR pack) — EM and PM both; snapshots currently die in a 2h in-memory Map
- Capacity allocation: how much went to roadmap vs bugs vs rework vs unplanned — EM and PM both, from JIRA issuetype + components + the existing rework detection
- Demote Mindwalk and Agent Teams out of the top-level sidebar — EM, PM, Lead and IC all called them demos competing with real work
- Cross-repo CI health (main-branch failure rate + flakes) — EM and Lead; `/api/ci/runs` is currently hardcoded to one workflow file in one project
- Explicit boundary, stated by EM, Lead AND IC without prompting: never ingest another engineer's transcripts, tokens or active hours. Measure the work, not the keystrokes

### Conflicts resolved

- **Manager visibility vs developer privacy — the hard one.** EM wants burnout signals and key-person risk; Lead wants per-engineer 1:1 cards; IC says any panel reading a teammate's transcripts, token spend or active hours makes him close the tab forever. The repo's transcript parser is powerful enough to build exactly the thing he fears.
  - *Resolution:* Two data planes, enforced in code, not in policy. PLANE A (work artifacts: JIRA, GitHub PRs, reviews, CI, bugs) is already team-visible — per-person views are allowed here, because the engineer can open every underlying artifact themselves. PLANE B (transcripts, tokens, cost, session times) is self-only: `server.mjs` only ever reads the local `~/.claude`, and we add a hard rule — no endpoint accepts a machine/user parameter for transcript data, and no aggregate over Plane B is ever keyed by a person other than the viewer. The join between planes (branch → ticket, for AI ROI) is permitted ONLY at cohort level, never at person level: the ROI endpoint drops the author field before aggregating. Written into the repo as a comment block at the top of `server-eng.mjs` and enforced by a test in `test/` that fails if `/api/roi` or `/api/eng/*` ever emits a per-person token or cost field.
- **EM's sustainable-pace / after-hours panel vs IC's "do not build an active-hours surveillance panel".** Both are right: after-hours load climbing for three weeks is a real signal a manager should see; a per-person after-hours chart is a stick.
  - *Resolution:* Team-aggregate only, from PR createdAt/mergedAt (a public artifact — NOT transcripts, which is what IC actually feared). Hard rules baked into the endpoint, not the UI: min-N suppression at 5 contributors (cell renders "—" below that), weekly buckets only, no per-person rows, no sort, no ranking, no drilldown from the chart to a person. The only action is "add to 1:1 agenda", which writes a dated note for the team's lead via the existing `/api/notes` — it names the team and the trend, never a person. If someone later asks for a per-person breakdown, the answer is that the endpoint physically cannot produce one.
- **Lead's per-engineer 1:1 prep card vs IC's "no per-person panel".** Lead needs three checkable things per report; IC will not tolerate a dossier.
  - *Resolution:* Symmetry rule: a per-person card ships only if the person themselves can render the identical card about themselves from the same UI, and it is built exclusively from Plane A artifacts (shipped tickets, PRs authored/reviewed, review findings received, escaped bugs, reopens, current WIP). No composite score, no ranking, no sort-by-output — rows are alphabetical. No token, cost, hours or transcript field appears anywhere on it. Default view is self. The card's only action is "Start 1:1 note" — it does not persist a running tally anyone can screenshot. Ships AFTER the review queue and CI strip, so trust is earned on unblocking work before anything person-shaped appears.
- **IC's "shipped ledger" (proof of my impact) vs the same data becoming a persistent "Ali: 4,231 lines" surveillance tile.**
  - *Resolution:* Ship it as a query, not a counter. It is a date-range you choose, rendered on demand, ending in one "copy as markdown" button — never a tile on Overview, never a stored value, never cross-person. This is the Career dashboard's existing job (`server-career.mjs` already writes brag docs and retros), so it stays there rather than getting a new home in the main shell.
- **PM planning view vs Lead actionable view.** PM wants funnels, burn-ups, allocation, commitment traffic-lights. Lead wants one row per piece of work with buttons on it. Building both as separate sections duplicates the pipeline and creates the fourth shell we just deleted.
  - *Resolution:* One Delivery section, one snapshot, two tabs off the same data. "Now" = operational: review-aging queue, in-flight ticket→PR→CI trace board, red CI, each row ending in Review / Nudge / Assign reviewer / Rerun. "Plan" = analytical: commitment vs completion, idea→prod funnel, allocation mix, trends, each chart ending in Export / Copy narrative / Set target. Both read `/api/eng/snapshot?key=all`; neither gets its own fetch path. *(Per the user constraint, these are two tabs of one Delivery page and both are always reachable — no lens toggle picks a default.)*
- **Inbox signal-to-noise:** EM wants delivery risk in it, IC wants his failed runs and hook blocks in it. Merged naively it becomes wallpaper and everyone stops reading it.
  - *Resolution:* Keep the existing `{key,kind,severity,text,ts,section}` shape and add a `plane` field (`'work' | 'harness'`). The Inbox gets two filter chips defaulting to both, severity-sorted as today. "Snooze 24h" extends the existing `meta.inboxDone` map with an expiry timestamp instead of a boolean, so a nagging item can be deferred rather than permanently cleared — the current binary clear is what will otherwise turn it into wallpaper.

### Cut

- **REJECTED BY USER — the Now/Plan/Me lens toggle** proposed in the thesis and in roadmap #2. No view/lens/role switcher ships in this dashboard. The Delivery section keeps its Now/Plan tabs as ordinary always-reachable tabs of one page, the harness panels live on one Harness page, and Overview shows the delivery/attention tiles to everyone. The two DATA planes (work vs harness) remain and are unaffected — they are a server-side boundary, not a UI mode.
- DELETE the gamification layer entirely — Pilot Level, XP bar, 🔥 streak, the 10 achievement badges (Overview.jsx:80-113 + the gami-strip), and the "Lv N · 🔥Nd" topbar chip (App.jsx ~204-214). All four personas asked for this independently. It is worse than decoration: XP is all-time assistant MESSAGE COUNT, so the fastest way to level up is a long, thrashing, unproductive conversation — the metric rewards exactly the behaviour the tool should reduce. And a token-count level plus a streak is one product decision away from being a per-engineer leaderboard, at which point every engineer stops trusting every number on the screen. `src/Gamification.jsx` moves to the Career dashboard, off by default.
- DEMOTE the Setup-health ring, the "Level"/"Specificity" columns and the "Quality distribution" panel (Overview.jsx). All three render the same static frontmatter heuristic. That is a linter, not a metric: a "perfect" 92-scored skill that has never fired is worthless, and a scruffy 41-scored skill invoked ten times a day is the most valuable file on disk. Replaced by roadmap #5 (fires × cost). The linter itself moves into Capabilities as an authoring aid.
- DEMOTE "cache saved $" (Overview.jsx:204) out of the KPI row to a tooltip on a real spend panel. It is an estimate (90% of input price) multiplied by an estimate (~4 chars/token), measured against a counterfactual that never happened, and it only ever goes up. No decision hangs on it.
- DEMOTE the Inventory table off the landing page into Capabilities. Harness authoring is real work; it is not what a landing page should open with.
- DEMOTE tool-usage-all-time bars, most-used-models bars, and the 18-week output-token heatmap into Chat Insights / the Harness page. The heatmap in particular is a GitHub-green-squares clone measuring token volume — a proxy for "was he typing", and the first panel anyone would screenshot to judge someone.
- RERANK "Top projects" — it currently sorts by output tokens, which rewards whichever project made Claude write the most text. Rank by sessions or commits.
- MOVE Mindwalk (3D repo replay) and Agent Teams + Team Designer into a single collapsed "Labs" sidebar entry. Both are demos; Agent Teams is explicitly Experimental and costs money per run; "Teams" also collides head-on with the word that means 25 humans to a manager. Rename to Agent Squads if it survives. Neither should outrank delivery data in a 15-item sidebar.
- DISSOLVE the four-shell portal (`?dash=cursor` / `eng` / `career`). The split IS the defect: the only genuinely team-wide data in the repo is behind the chip nobody clicks. Eng folds into the shell as the Delivery section (#2). ## Engineering Metrics Dashboard

*`src/EngDashboard.jsx` (1,282 lines) + `server-eng.mjs` (688 lines). JIRA REST v3 (Basic auth from `.eng.local.json`, or acli's OAuth token from the macOS keychain) + GitHub via the authed `gh` CLI (GraphQL, 300 most-recent PRs). Multi-project (AIR + TRN, extensible via projects.json). A genuinely strong working-time engine (10:00-18:00 Sun-Thu, Asia/Riyadh) drives every duration; per-issue metrics are reconstructed from the JIRA changelog. Snapshot cached 2h. Routes today: Overview · Members · Board · Sprint · Workload · Review · OKRs · Arcade.*

**Thesis** — This dashboard's job is to shorten the distance between a delivery signal and the person who can act on it — and it must do that without ever becoming a weapon pointed at the engineers who generate the data. Today it is an excellent team-lead forensics tool wearing a manager's clothes: it reconstructs the working-time history of any single ticket beautifully, and then answers none of the four questions its four audiences actually open it to ask. The rebuild is organised around one insight that shows up in all four reviews independently: nearly every signal these people want is ALREADY COMPUTED and thrown away — `rec.atRisk`, `i.stale`, `reviewEvents`, `activeDays`/`waitDays`, `f.parent`, `f.labels`, the Sprint changelog items sitting next to the status ones. Almost nothing here needs a new data source; it needs the data to stop hiding inside a hover tooltip. So: the Attention Queue lands (one ranked list of interventions, filterable to "mine", with a real action on every row), percentiles replace means everywhere (the mean is the number that hides the escalation), and the review queue, the escape rate and the investment mix get built out of fields the server already ships. The dashboard must not alienate the ICs — and the way it currently would is not subtle: a "Delivery skills" radar scoring five invented composites 0-100 per person, a "Bugs owned — he caused" counter built on who happened to drag a card, and a tooltip that literally coaches engineers to re-point finished tickets so the estimation metric goes green. All four personas, including both managers, demanded those be cut, and they are cut — not hidden behind a flag, deleted. What replaces them is a firm contract: team-level aggregates and per-person OPERATIONAL facts are public because they help people coordinate; per-person EVALUATIVE scores do not exist; and every number the dashboard holds about an engineer, that engineer can export about themselves. Get that contract right and the metrics stay honest, because nobody has a reason to game them. Get it wrong and every other number on this page becomes fiction within a quarter.

### Consensus

- Aging WIP / stalled-work queue as a single ranked list with an action per row — all FOUR personas asked for this independently (EM "Attention queue", PM "Aging WIP / stalled queue", Lead "Standup Queue", IC "My Queue"). Every ingredient (`rec.atRisk`, `rec.budget/remaining`, `i.stale/staleNote`, `inCurrent`, `curSince`, `prs[].openDays`, `prs[].reviewEvents`) is already computed per-ticket today and then buried inside an IssueTip hover tooltip on a kanban card — the one place nobody looks.
- p50/p90 percentiles and distributions everywhere a mean is shown — EM, PM, Lead and IC all said the mean is the number that lies. There is not one percentile in the entire codebase; `avg()` (EngDashboard.jsx:15) is the only statistic.
- Review-flow aggregation BY REVIEWER, not by PR — EM (reviewer concentration / burnout), Lead (whose queue is the bottleneck), IC (which reviewer is the black hole, who do I route to). `prs[].reviewEvents {state, login, at}` is fetched, shipped in the snapshot, and aggregated by nobody. `reviewRequests` (who was ASKED but never responded) is not in the GraphQL query at all — that is the actual missing field.
- Escaped-defect rate + area/component hotspots to REPLACE the per-person blame panels — EM, PM and Lead all made the same argument: bug LOAD by area is a capacity and hardening argument; bug ratio by person is an artifact that teaches people to stop linking bugs to their parent story, destroying the very data the panel is built on.
- An export/copy artifact as the terminal action of every panel — EM (quarter brief for the VP), PM (weekly stakeholder digest), Lead (standup summary to Slack), IC (brag doc incl. reviews given). Four different documents, one generator. Nothing in the app exports anything today.
- Investment / effort mix — where the engineering days actually went (feature vs bug vs toil vs rework) — EM ("bug tax") and PM ("effort mix"). `issue.pts`, `.type`, `.isBug`, `.rework`, `.activeDays` and `.area` all exist and are never summed for this purpose; `f.labels` is even in the JIRA FIELDS request and then dropped from the payload.
- Sprint commitment vs delivered, carryover and mid-sprint injection — EM (predictability, what he is accountable for upward) and PM (the only honest answer to "did engineering miss or did I keep adding?"). The changelog is ALREADY fetched with `expand=changelog` and `historyOf()` (server-eng.mjs:543) proves `field==='Sprint'` items are sitting right next to the status items that `statusSegments()` reads.
- Kill the calendar-month-only lens in favour of sprint/quarter/rolling windows — PM and IC, and implicitly the EM via the OKR quarter bug.

### Conflicts resolved

- **Manager visibility vs developer privacy.** The EM wants to know "who is drowning" and "is anyone burning out"; the Lead wants per-person 1:1 material; the IC's verbatim complaint is "this is not a dashboard for me — it's a dashboard about me". Today the app ships per-person EVALUATIVE scores (memberRadar's Grooming/Planning/Estimation/DevQuality/Release, Workload's "Bugs owned — he caused" and "Bug ratio — caused/tickets") built on guessed attribution: owner defaults to the parent story's current assignee, fixer is whoever dragged the card to QA-ready (server-eng.mjs:262). All four personas independently demanded these be cut.
  - *Resolution:* A three-tier data contract, enforced in the snapshot, not by convention. TIER A (team aggregate, visible to everyone, the default for every panel): cycle p50/p90, escape rate, review-wait SLA, investment mix, WIP-per-engineer as a ratio, after-hours load. TIER B (per-person OPERATIONAL facts, visible to everyone, because they are already public in JIRA/GitHub and are needed to unblock work today): who owns which open ticket, whose review queue a PR is sitting in, whose PR is stale — coordination facts, not judgments; no scoring, no ranking, no leaderboard sort. TIER C (per-person EVALUATIVE/derived): DELETED, not hidden — memberRadar, bug ratio, "bugs he caused", the owner→fixer gossip board, per-person after-hours, per-person AI cost, all removed from the codebase. The only per-person evaluative surface that survives is the 1:1 Pack, which is (a) self-authored by the lead, (b) persisted to a gitignored `eng-1on1.json`, (c) never rendered on any team-visible route, and (d) mirrored to the engineer via the Impact Export, so nothing exists about a person that they cannot pull about themselves. Enforcement: a k-anonymity guard in the snapshot — any per-person derived aggregate with n<5 tickets renders "—", and the sustainable-pace panel refuses to render a per-engineer column at all. Identity comes from `gh api user` + the atlassian email in `.eng.local.json`, so "me" is a real filter, not a dropdown of colleagues.
- **PM planning view vs Lead/IC actionable view.** Both want an "aging WIP" surface, but the PM wants it ranked by date risk to a commitment and the Lead wants it ranked by severity and age with a button on every row. Building both is how a dashboard becomes a landfill of near-duplicate lists — the repo already has this disease (Board and Sprint are both `ColumnsBoard` over sprint-grouped dev-scoped issues, near-identical).
  - *Resolution:* One signal engine, two renderings — never two computations. A single server-side `triage()` pass emits typed risk records `{kind, severity, subject, owner, ageWorkDays, overBudgetBy, deepLink, suggestedAction}` for every stall. The Attention Queue (landing route) renders these rows sorted by severity × age with an action per row. The Delivery Forecast renders the SAME records rolled up to the epic/sprint they belong to, expressed as days of date risk against the epic's `duedate`. Same records, same colours, one source of truth — if the queue is empty the forecast is green, by construction. ## Career Dashboard

*`src/CareerDashboard.jsx`, `src/career/*`, `server-career.mjs`, `server-eng.mjs`, `career-*.mjs`*

**Thesis** — This app already fetches everything four roles need and then throws ~90% of it away: `loadEngSelf` (src/career/data.jsx:68) pulls the entire team snapshot — every issue with assignee/rec.atRisk/daysIn, every PR with reviewers/reviewEvents/files — and keeps only rows where `assignee.id === accountId`; the career shell never once calls `/api/flow`, `/api/harness`, `/api/gov/costs`, `/api/gov/failures` or `/api/memory/search` even though they run in the same process. What ships is a SMALLER app: five pages (Now / Delivery / Team / Harness / Review-season), every number clickable to its evidence, every panel ending in a button that writes a file, posts via gh, or fills the clipboard.

## Cursor Dashboard

*`src/CursorDashboard.jsx` + `server-cursor.mjs`*

**Thesis** — This is the dashboard for the AI harness and its outcomes — nothing else. It answers one question at four altitudes: "is the money and context we pour into Cursor coming back out as merged code?" The IC sees why their sessions fail (tool errors, context cliffs, always-context tax) and what they shipped. The lead sees which repo's harness is broken and which AI-heavy PRs cost extra review cycles. The PM sees cost per shipped ticket. The EM sees seat utilization and spend-vs-throughput. The spine that makes all four possible is ONE join — session → git commit → branch → ticket → PR — built once, rendered at four levels of aggregation. It must not alienate the developer: the moment this becomes a per-person lines-of-AI-code leaderboard or a when-is-Ali-online grid, engineers route around it and every number above becomes a lie. So the hard rule is architectural, not cultural: local full-fidelity data (transcripts, prompts, timing, hour-of-day) never leaves the machine and is only ever self-scoped; team data comes exclusively from the Admin API and GitHub/JIRA — artifacts the org already owns — and per-person rows exist only for three administrative facts (seat assigned, client version, spend). Everything behavioral is k≥5 aggregate, enforced in the server, not the client. It must also not become the eng dashboard: PR queues, JIRA cycle time and defect escape already live in `src/EngDashboard.jsx`. This one links out.

### Conflicts resolved

- **Manager visibility vs developer privacy.** The EM and lead need per-engineer adoption/spend data to justify a five-figure bill; the IC and lead both explicitly refuse a per-dev AI-lines leaderboard, and every persona flagged the transcript reader and hour-of-day heatmap as surveillance-shaped.
  - *Resolution:* A hard SERVER-SIDE boundary, not a UI convention. Two data planes. (1) LOCAL plane — everything read from `state.vscdb` (transcripts, prompt text, timing, hour-of-day, tool errors, cursorBlame) is self-only, always: never keyed by anyone else's email, never uploaded, no team aggregate. Stays under `/api/cursor/*`. (2) TEAM plane — a new `server-cursor-team.mjs` is the ONLY module allowed to serve multi-person data, and it may read exactly two sources: the Cursor Admin API and the GitHub/JIRA snapshot server-eng.mjs already fetches. It emits per-email rows for exactly three administrative facts a manager legitimately owns — seat assigned/unused, client version, spend — and NOTHING else. Every behavioral metric (session counts, accept rate, weekend activity, latency, consecutive active days, AI lines) is aggregated to repo-or-team level with a k≥5 floor BEFORE it leaves the server, so the route physically cannot return a per-email behavioral row and no future UI change can leak one. The weekend signal ships as a team percentage plus "N engineers with 6+ consecutive active days" as a count, never a list. The transcript reader stays, IC-only, on the local plane. That is the trade: the manager gets the seat/spend accountability they are actually judged on and gives up individual behavioral telemetry they do not need — and if they later want it, it is a code change someone has to defend in review, not a checkbox.
- **EM wants "AI ship rate" (accepted AI lines / merged-PR additions) segmented by project as a headline; the IC warns any such number becomes a per-dev productivity grade the moment someone screenshots it.**
  - *Resolution:* Ship rate is computed and stored ONLY at repo/week granularity — the join key is (repo, week), never (email, week), so there is no per-person value in existence to leak. And it never renders alone: every ship-rate reading appears beside that repo/week's median review cycles, because a rising ship rate with rising review cycles is a warning, not a win. The EM's own threshold framing (">60% = flag for a review-depth conversation") is baked into the UI copy so the metric cannot be read as higher-is-better.
- **PM wants planning rollups (cost per shipped ticket, effort mix, cohort cycle-time comparison); the lead wants a per-PR actionable table.** Building both as separate pipelines is exactly how this becomes a landfill.
  - *Resolution:* One join engine, two renderings. `server-cursor-join.mjs` owns the single pipeline: session (folder, createdAt..lastUpdatedAt, codeBlockData file paths) → `git log --since --until --name-only` in that folder → commit + branch → ticket key via the SAME `cfg.ticketRegex` server-eng.mjs already applies → PR/JIRA row from the existing 2h-cached eng snapshot. It emits ONE row type: `{session, commit, branch, ticket, pr, cost, aiLines}`. The PM view is a GROUP BY over those rows (by ticket type, by week, by cohort). The lead view is the raw rows filtered to merged PRs, sorted by review cycles. Zero duplicate fetching, zero duplicate join logic, and when the join is wrong it is wrong in exactly one place. Both views refuse to render a comparison below n=20 per bucket and print the n.
- **PM wants a monthly-budget threshold raising an Inbox notification; the IC wants no cost panel at all** ("I'd rather have no cost panel than a pipe from my machine to someone else's report").
  - *Resolution:* The IC's objection rests on a factual error — they believed spend required the org Admin API. It does not: `usageData` with `costInCents` is in the LOCAL DB on 310 sessions (verified). So the spend panel is local-plane: it shows the signed-in user their own spend and never transmits. The team spend figure the EM and PM need comes from the Admin API's `/teams/spend` — billing data the org already has and never needed a developer's laptop to obtain. Nothing flows upward from a machine. The budget-threshold Inbox alert fires for the person whose budget it is.
- **IC and lead both want a "dead rules" panel driven by `cursorRules` fire counts. The data does not support it.**
  - *Resolution:* Dropped as specified, rebuilt honestly. Ground truth: exactly 4 distinct paths have EVER appeared in `cursorRules` across the whole DB, and all four are `~/.claude` or `~/.cursor` plugin SKILL.md files — Cursor does not record .mdc rule application at all in this build. A "fires: 0" column would mark every project rule dead and be flatly wrong. So the panel ships as always-context TAX (tokens paid on every single chat — knowable, real, already computed by `harness()`/`listResources()`) plus a clearly labelled "recorded activations (plugin skills only — Cursor does not log .mdc rule loads)" column. Same delete/demote actions, honest evidence underneath them.
